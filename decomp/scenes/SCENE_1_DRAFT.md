## THE BRIDGE DOESN'T EXIST YET

*A bullpen on a Thursday, 7:40pm. Two monitors, both awake. On the left, a
workbook open to a single populated tab —* Assumptions *— and eight empty ones
behind it. On the right, a PDF of a quality-of-earnings report, scrolled to a
page of adjustments.*

MARIA HALVORSEN, *an associate, mid-thirties, jacket over the back of a chair
that isn't hers. She has been standing for a while. She is talking to the desk
beside her, which is empty, and to the recorder on her phone, which is not.*

---

**MARIA**

The QoE came back at four.

*(she scrolls it, doesn't read it)*

Which means the model goes in the data room Monday, and what we have is one
sheet. Assumptions. Everything downstream got stripped out in the rebuild and
nobody put it back, so — that's the evening.

Four tabs. Off that sheet. FY2022A through FY2030P, all four of them, every
year. Enterprise P&L. Working Capital. Debt Schedule. And a bridge, FY2024A to
FY2025B.

Use what's already in there — the structure, the units, the formatting. This is
a live deal. Nobody gets to invent a house style at eight o'clock on a Thursday.

*(she sits)*

The P&L first, because everything else hangs off it. Through Net Income.
Operating assumptions link back to the Assumptions sheet. Debt interest links
forward to the Debt Schedule, which doesn't exist yet, so you build the shell
and point at it and it fills in when you get there.

Lines, in order. Depreciation and Amortization. Operating Income — EBIT. Then
the QoE adjustments, because that's the whole reason we're doing this tonight.
Adjusted EBITDA. Interest Expense. Other Income and Expense. Tax Expense. Net
Income. Margins alongside, all the way down.

Non-operating other income is given. Four point eight. Four point eight. Four
point seven. Four point four. Three point nine. Four point two. Four point
three. Four point five. Four point seven. FY2022A through FY2030P, in that
order, nine years, nine numbers.

Tax on positive pre-tax income only. Not on losses. At the consolidated
effective rate — it's on the Assumptions sheet, don't type it in again.

*(she pulls up the second monitor, blank)*

Working capital. Three-sixty-five-day basis.

DSO — forty-two point seven days in FY2022A. Forty-two point five in FY2023A
and again in FY2024A. Forty-two point oh from FY2025B onward, and it holds.

DIH — sixty point oh in FY2022A. Fifty-two in FY2023A. Forty-nine in FY2024A.
Forty-eight from FY2025B onward.

DPO — a hundred and five in FY2022A. Ninety-four in FY2023A. A hundred and ten
in FY2024A — that one's real, that's the year they stretched their payables,
don't smooth it — then back to a hundred and five from FY2025B on.

Prepaids, zero percent of operating expenses. Accrued liabilities, ten percent
of operating expenses. Deferred revenue, zero percent of revenue.

*(a beat)*

Debt. Two tranches, and interest on the average of beginning and ending
balances — average, both tranches, every year. Not on the opening balance.
They'll check.

The term loan opens at three point five in FY2022A. Mandatory repayments of
nought point five a year, starting in FY2022A, and they keep going until it's
gone. Rate is zero percent in FY2022A and FY2023A — zero, that's not a
placeholder, that's the deal — then ten percent from FY2024A onward, for as
long as there's a balance outstanding.

The line of credit opens at one point five in FY2022A. Repayments of nought
point five in FY2023A. Nought point five in FY2024A. Nought point three in
FY2025B. Then nothing — no further draws, no further repayments — which leaves
nought point two sitting there, outstanding, all the way through FY2030P. Ten
percent throughout, FY2022A to FY2030P, the whole way.

*(she turns the QoE face down)*

And the bridge. FY2024A EBITDA to FY2025B EBITDA.

This is the page the sponsor turns to first. Not the P&L. This one. So it is
formula-driven, every step of it — if I click a cell in that walk and it's a
number, we start over.

Separate variance step for each of: Segment Alpha revenue. Segment Beta
revenue. Segment Gamma revenue. Everything else on the revenue side collapsed
into one line, and call it "Other Revenue Lines" — those words. Then COGS.
Customer Acquisition. Postal Charges. Staff Costs. SG&A. Other OpEx.

Columns: the component. The period-over-period change in value. The running
cumulative EBITDA as you walk. And the component type.

Underneath it, a helper block for the waterfall chart — Base, Increase,
Decrease, Total — formula-driven as well, ready to plot, because somebody will
want that chart in a deck by Sunday and it won't be me building it twice.

Then three lines to close it. Starting EBITDA. Total change. Ending EBITDA.

*(she looks at the empty tabs)*

Everything links. To the Assumptions sheet, and to each other. Keep the colour
convention — typed inputs one colour, formulas another, cross-sheet links a
third, the way the rest of the book already does it.

And it ties out before it comes to me. All of it. Because if the bridge doesn't
foot to the P&L, I'm not the one who finds out.

*(beat)*

The sponsor is.

*She reaches over and closes the empty tab of the workbook that isn't hers.*
