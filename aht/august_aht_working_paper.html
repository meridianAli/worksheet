How to read the sort

The order is honest but the top and bottom of it are not informative. Both ends are dominated by people with one to four credited tasks, where a single task decides the whole ranking: Mitchell Kimball at 0.15h and Winston Curteza at 8.12h each have exactly one task. A median over one number is that number.

Twenty-five people cleared 10 tasks. Inside that group the spread is 0.32h (Alex Fey, 30 tasks) to 3.19h (Andy Deng, 10 tasks) — still roughly 10× inside a single job title, and that is the real finding. The people worth acting on first are the ones who are both slow and high-volume, because they carry the most hours: Melvan Hoo Jun Hong (2.33h median × 42 tasks, $200/h), Liam Tuohy (2.86h × 25), Ethan Ohana (2.17h × 34) and Tyler Collingbourne (2.02h × 17). Between them that is about 118 tasks running roughly an hour over cohort median, which is on the order of $18k–$20k a month of avoidable time.

At the other end, Alex Fey (0.32h over 30 tasks) and Daniel Zalevsky (0.61h over 21) are fast enough that they are worth checking rather than celebrating — verify their audit pass rate before holding them up as the target, since this metric measures time to reach audit, not quality once there.

The eleven with no credited task

Rows 50–60 booked 43.1 payable hours and $6,517 in August — 2.4% of the cohort's August cost — without a single task reaching audit. Most logged one to five active days at small hours, which reads as onboarding or ramp rather than leakage. Troye Bullock is the one row that looks different: 10 active days spread across the month for 4.4 hours and still nothing credited, which is worth a direct question rather than an assumption.

Reconciliation: 59 people had August payable hours, 48 of them were credited with a task, 11 were not. One further person (Daurys gonell) has a credited task but no August payable time — 48 + 11 + 1 = 60 rows.

02The work, step by step

Everything below was run read-only against tsip_prd through the Metabase dataset API. Each step gives the query, the result, and any judgement call it forced.

Step 1 · define the population

Who counts as a “prompt-only tasker”?

First establish whether anyone holds more than one role, since the brief was to exclude them. 

SELECT m.role::text, count(*) AS rows_, count(DISTINCT m.user_id) AS users
FROM tsip_project_members m
WHERE m.project_id = 'sheets'
GROUP BY m.role;

-- and, to prove nobody holds two roles:
SELECT c, count(*) FROM (
  SELECT user_id, count(*) AS c FROM tsip_project_members
  WHERE project_id='sheets' GROUP BY 1
) x GROUP BY c;
Result. 618 sheets members. Every one has exactly one role row — the “rows per user” query returns a single bucket, 1 → 618. So multiple roles do not occur in this data and nobody was excluded on that basis.

Roles: beginner_tasker 369 · prompt_tasker 112 · calibration_tasker 90 · reviewer 19 · decomp_prompt_tasker 10 · calibration_failed 7 · decomp_prompt_reviewer 3 · decomp_tasker 3 · beginner_tasker_reviewer 3 · eval_hallucination_reviewer 2.

Judgement call: the filter is role = 'prompt_tasker' exactly. This drops the hybrids (decomp_prompt_tasker, decomp_prompt_reviewer) as instructed — and also drops people doing prompt work under a reviewer or beginner title. Most consequentially it excludes the project's highest-volume contributor, who is beginner_tasker_reviewer. The role field is a permissions label, not a description of work.

Step 2 · define the task set

Unique tasks that reached audit in August

“Unique” is enforced by taking each task's first entry into audit — tasks can re-enter, and counting entries instead of tasks would double-count. Day bucketing is America/New_York via the double convert, per sheets ground truth (DB timestamps are naive UTC; a single convert shifts the day boundary).

Result. 1,287 unique tasks first entered audit between 2026-08-01 and 2026-08-31 ET, across all roles.
Step 3 · the credit rule

Who does a task belong to?

The obvious reading — whoever triggered the transition into audit — turns out to be wrong. I checked it before relying on it: 

WITH tp AS (
  SELECT t.id AS task_id FROM tsip_tasks t
  JOIN tsip_project_versions pv ON pv.id = t.project_version_id
  WHERE pv.project_id = 'sheets')
SELECT DISTINCT ON (tst.task_id)
       tst.task_id, tst.created_at, tst.triggered_by, tst.from_state, tst.event
FROM tsip_state_transitions tst JOIN tp ON tp.task_id = tst.task_id
WHERE tst.to_state = 'audit' AND tst.from_state IS DISTINCT FROM 'audit'
ORDER BY tst.task_id, tst.created_at;
-- then group the August rows by the trigger's role
Result — August audit entries by trigger role.
(no membership row) via review_gate → APPROVE   1,248  (97.0%)
eval_hallucination_reviewer via SEND_FORWARD_TO_AUDIT   29
beginner_tasker_reviewer via APPROVE   4
reviewer via ADMIN_OVERRIDE   3
decomp_prompt_reviewer via APPROVE   1
prompt_tasker   2  (both ADMIN_OVERRIDE)

97% of tasks are pushed into audit by a system account. Taking the trigger literally would return two tasks for the entire month. So credit follows the sheets ground-truth convention instead: the last person to move the task out of edit_task or redo_task before it was approved — whoever's work led it there. 

cred AS (
  SELECT fa.task_id, fa.audit_at,
    (SELECT e.triggered_by
       FROM tsip_state_transitions e
      WHERE e.task_id = fa.task_id
        AND e.from_state IN ('edit_task','redo_task')
        AND e.from_state IS DISTINCT FROM e.to_state
        AND e.created_at <= fa.audit_at
      ORDER BY e.created_at DESC
      LIMIT 1) AS user_id
  FROM fa)
SELECT c.task_id, c.user_id
FROM cred c
JOIN tsip_project_members m
  ON m.user_id = c.user_id AND m.project_id = 'sheets'
WHERE m.role::text = 'prompt_tasker';
Result. Of 1,287 August tasks, 907 are credited to a contributor whose role is exactly prompt_tasker. The remaining 380 belong to the other nine roles and are out of scope.
Step 4 · measure time on the task

Time on that task specifically, not allocated

There is no per-task time field in the platform — payable time is project-grain. The only per-task signal is tsip_task_claims: each row is one person holding one task, with claimed_at and last_activity_at. I sum the credited person's claims on that exact task, capping each claim at 12 hours so one forgotten claim cannot dominate. 

SELECT pt.task_id, pt.user_id,
  sum(LEAST(GREATEST(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (
        COALESCE(c.last_activity_at, c.closed_at, c.claimed_at) - c.claimed_at
      ))/3600.0, 0), 12) )
    FILTER (WHERE c.user_id = pt.user_id)        AS own_h,
  sum(LEAST(GREATEST(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (
        COALESCE(c.last_activity_at, c.closed_at, c.claimed_at) - c.claimed_at
      ))/3600.0, 0), 12) )                        AS all_h,
  count(c.id) FILTER (WHERE c.user_id = pt.user_id) AS claims_own
FROM pt LEFT JOIN tsip_task_claims c ON c.task_id = pt.task_id
GROUP BY 1, 2;
Result — raw claim-hold hours. 907 tasks, of which 905 have a claim record for the credited person (2 do not and are dropped).
mean 16.76 claim-hours · median 12.89 · total 15,171
counting every contributor's claims rather than just the credited one: mean 21.55, median 17.09

These raw numbers are not work hours. A claim is wall-clock possession — people keep a task claimed overnight and over weekends. 16.76 hours per task is obviously not what anyone worked. Step 5 fixes this.

Step 5 · calibrate

Convert possession time into paid work time

Pull what these same people were actually paid for in August — a completely independent source that never touches the claims table. 

-- independent of claims entirely: what these people were actually paid for
WITH pt AS (SELECT user_id FROM tsip_project_members
            WHERE project_id='sheets' AND role::text='prompt_tasker'),
rev AS (SELECT DISTINCT ON (ptr.payable_time_entry_id)
               ptr.id, ptr.total_seconds, ptr.started_at, pte.user_id
        FROM payable_time_revisions ptr
        JOIN payable_time_entries pte ON pte.id = ptr.payable_time_entry_id
        WHERE ptr.project_id='sheets' AND ptr.started_at >= '2026-07-28'
        ORDER BY ptr.payable_time_entry_id, ptr.revision_number DESC)
SELECT sum(s.total_seconds)/3600.0 AS aug_payable_hours
FROM rev r
JOIN payable_time_revision_segments s ON s.revision_id = r.id
WHERE (s.started_at AT TIME ZONE 'UTC' AT TIME ZONE 'America/New_York')::date
      BETWEEN '2026-08-01' AND '2026-08-31'
  AND r.user_id IN (SELECT user_id FROM pt)
  AND r.user_id NOT IN (SELECT id FROM internal);
Result. 59 prompt_tasker accounts booked 1,789.1 payable hours in August, costing $291,936 — a blended $163.17/hour.

The calibration is then one division:

calibration factor = 1,789.1 payable hours / 15,171 claim-hours
                   = 0.11793

mean   16.76 claim-h  x 0.11793 = 1.98 work hours  -> $323/task
median 12.89 claim-h  x 0.11793 = 1.52 work hours  -> $248/task

Note what this does: it makes the mean exact by construction — 1,789.1 hours ÷ 907 tasks = 1.97h, which is the mean with no modelling in it at all. The claims data is only doing one job: distributing that known total across tasks so a median and a distribution can exist.

Step 6 · check it

Does the answer survive scrutiny?

  • Independent reconciliation. The top-down figure (payable hours ÷ tasks = 1.97h) and the bottom-up scaled mean (1.98h) agree. They share the numerator by construction, so this checks the task count, not the hours.
  • Against the wider pipeline. An earlier, separate calculation over Jul–Aug and all roles gave a blended AHT of 2.46h. August prompt-only at 1.98h is lower, which is the right direction: that figure included decomp, calibration and beginner time.
  • Cap sensitivity. With no per-claim cap, raw mean rises to 50.5 claim-hours and median to 22.6 — the calibration absorbs the mean shift entirely, but the median would move. This is the single largest uncertainty in the median and is stated rather than hidden.
  • Coverage. 905 of 907 tasks (99.8%) have claim data. The 2 without are dropped rather than counted as zero, which would have dragged the median down.
  • Row limits. The Metabase dataset API truncates at 2,000 rows. Every distribution statistic here is computed server-side with percentile_cont, so nothing is silently cut. This bit me earlier in the project and is worth knowing.

03What could still be wrong

  • Claim-hold is a proxy for effort, not a measurement of it. Someone who claims a task, works 20 minutes, and releases it 6 hours later looks identical to someone who worked 6 hours. Across 905 tasks this should average out; for any single task it may be badly wrong. Do not use a per-task figure to judge a person.
  • The 12-hour cap is a judgement, not a measurement. It is the main lever on the median.
  • Credit goes to the last submitter before audit. A task reassigned late gives all the credit to whoever finished it, even if someone else did most of the work. 92% of send-backs are self-fixed, so this is usually the same person — but not always.
  • The role filter excludes real prompt work done under reviewer or beginner titles, including the project's biggest producer. This is the requested scope, not an accident.
  • Everything is blended across task varieties. Patch-based still cannot be separated from greenfield in platform data.
  • One inconsistency worth noting: Daurys gonell is credited with an August task but has no August payable time, so their hours come from a claim that spans the month boundary. Immaterial at one task, but it is the kind of edge that shows the two data sources are not perfectly aligned.
